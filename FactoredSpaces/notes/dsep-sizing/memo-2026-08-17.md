# Sizing Proposition 5.5 (d-separation ⟺ structural independence) for Lean 4

**Paper:** Garrabrant, Mayer, Wache, Lang, Eisenstat, Dell, *Factored space models*, arXiv:2412.02579v2.
Source read: `FactoredSpaces/notes/2412.02579v2-main.tex` — §4 (lines 152–320), §5.2–5.3 (355–480), §6.1 (481–555), Appendix B.2–B.3 (922–1050).

**Verdict up front:** the direct route is **bounded and tractable**. Estimated **≈2 300 Lean lines specific to Prop 5.5** (range 1 600–3 600), on top of ≈550 lines of DAG/d-separation scaffolding that any formalization of §5 needs anyway. The paper's literal route (K–F soundness *and* completeness of d-separation) is **strictly worse and carries the only genuinely research-scale item in the neighbourhood** (completeness), so it should not be taken.

Everything below has been checked by brute force against the definitions (exhaustive enumeration of all `J ⊆ I` for five DAGs, all `Z`, all `z`, all node subsets; plus 650 k random d-separation triples). Scripts: `scratchpad/fsm.py`, `test3.py`, `test5.py`, `test7.py`, `test4.py`.

## 0. Setup and notation

Fix a DAG `G = (V,E,Val)` with `|Val_v| ≥ 2` for all `v` (the paper's standing assumption; §5.2 line 362). The FSM `M_G` has

* `I = ⨆_{v∈V} I_v`, `I_v = {(v, y) : y ∈ Val_pa(v)}`, `Ω_{(v,y)} = Val_v`,
* `X_v(ω) = ω_{(v, X_pa(v)(ω))}` (recursion well-founded by acyclicity),
* `H(X | C)` = least `J ⊆ I` that **disintegrates** `C` (`C = C_J × C_{I∖J}`, equivalently `C` is closed under splicing two of its members at `J`) and **determines** `X` on `C`.

Throughout `Z := V₃`, `z ∈ Val_Z`, `C := {ω : X_Z(ω) = z}`. Write `I_W := ⋃_{u∈W} I_u`.

Three definitions carry the whole answer.

> **Def A (unblocked ancestors).** `A_Z(v) := { u : ∃ directed path u = u₀ → … → u_k = v (k ≥ 0) with u₀,…,u_{k−1} ∉ Z }`.
> So `v ∈ A_Z(v)`, and `A_Z(v) ∖ {v}` is disjoint from `Z`. (Equivalently: ancestors-plus-self in `G` after deleting every edge *out of* a node of `Z`.)
>
> **Def B (Z-closure).** `S ⊆ V` is *Z-closed* if for every `w ∈ Z`, `A_Z(w) ∩ S ≠ ∅ ⟹ A_Z(w) ⊆ S`. For `s ∉ Z` let `S_Z(s)` be the least Z-closed set containing `A_Z(s)`; for `s ∈ Z` set `S_Z(s) := ∅`.
>
> **Def C (z-consistent indices).** `I^z := { (u,y) ∈ I : y_p = z_p for every p ∈ pa(u) ∩ Z }`, and `I^z_W := I^z ∩ I_W`. Note `I^z_u ≠ ∅` for every `u` (choose `y` freely off `Z`), and `I^z_u ≅ Val_{pa(u)∖Z}`.

`S_Z(s)` is precisely the "unblocked ancestor" set `AncZ*` of Zhang et al. (arXiv:2604.22041, Def. 3.2), closed under the collider-merging step.

## 1. The characterization

> **Theorem 1 (conditional history in `M_G`).** For every `A ⊆ V`, `Z ⊆ V`, `z ∈ Val_Z`,
> ```
> H(X_A | X_Z = z)  =  I^z_{ S_Z(A) },      S_Z(A) := ⋃_{a∈A} S_Z(a).
> ```
> In particular the *vertex* set `S_Z(A)` does not depend on `z`; only the per-node restriction `I^z_u` does.

Special cases: `Z = ∅` gives `H(X_v) = I_{An(v)∪{v}}`, the paper's claim in the proof of Prop 5.6; `v ∈ Z` gives `H(X_v | z) = ∅`; the chain `a→b→c` conditioned on `b = b₀` gives `H(X_a|b₀) = I_a ∪ I_b` (all of `I_b`) but `H(X_c|b₀) = {(c,b₀)}` (a *single* entry) — the asymmetry that makes the naive "reachable set" guess fail and forces the `I^z` restriction.

> **Corollary 2.** `X_{V₁} ⊥_Ω X_{V₂} | X_{V₃}` iff `S_Z(V₁) ∩ S_Z(V₂) = ∅`. The "for all `z`" quantifier in Def. 4.10 collapses: the condition is the same for every `z`, and `V₃ = ∅` needs no separate treatment.

**Verification.** Brute force over all `J ⊆ I` (disintegration checked as `|C| = |C_J|·|C_{I∖J}|`, which is exact since `C ⊆ C_J × C_{I∖J}` always): chain `a→b→c`, fork, collider `a→c←b`, collider+descendant, diamond, triangle, with binary and ternary value spaces, all `Z`, all `z`, all node subsets `A` — **0 mismatches** (1 400+ histories). With `|Val_b| = 1` the formula fails, confirming that `|Val_v| ≥ 2` is load-bearing and must appear as a hypothesis.

### 1.1 Proof (obligations, in dependency order)

Let `C = {X_Z = z}`, and abbreviate `T_u := A_Z(u)`, `S := S_Z(s)`.

**(L1) Realization.** `{X(ω) : ω ∈ C} = {x ∈ Val : x_Z = z}`. *Proof:* for `x` with `x_Z = z`, the constant table `ω_{(v,·)} ≡ x_v` has `X(ω) = x` (induction on a topological order). Easy; this is the workhorse for every "there exists a point of `C` with …" step.

**(L2) Only z-consistent entries are consulted.** For `ω ∈ C`, `(v, X_pa(v)(ω)) ∈ I^z` for all `v`; consequently `X` restricted to `C` factors through `π_{I^z}`, and `C = C_{I^z} × Ω_{I∖I^z}`. Hence `I^z` generates every `X_s` given `C`, and **`H(X_s|z) ⊆ I^z`**. *Proof:* induction on a topological order, using `X_p(ω) = z_p` for `p ∈ Z`.

**(L3) Determination.** For `s ∉ Z`: `J` determines `X_s` on `C` **iff** `J ⊇ I^z_{T_s}`.
*(⇐)* On `C`, `X_s` is a function of `π_{I^z_{T_s}}` (induction along `G`: the entry consulted at `u ∈ T_s` lies in `I^z_u`, and every non-`Z` parent of `u ∈ T_s` is again in `T_s`).
*(⇒)* Relevance: for each `i ∈ I^z_{T_s}` there are `ω, ω' ∈ C` differing only at `i` with `X_s(ω) ≠ X_s(ω')`. *Construction:* set every `w ∈ Z` to the constant table `z_w` (so `C`-membership is automatic and unaffected by anything else), realize the parent configuration of `i = (u,y)` by constants, and make each node on one directed path `u ⇝ s` a function of its predecessor only, separating two values (`|Val| ≥ 2`). Then flipping `ω_i` flips `X_s`. For `s ∈ Z`, `X_s ≡ z_s` on `C` and `H = ∅`.

**(L4) Blocks disintegrate (easy half of the lattice analysis).** If `S` is Z-closed and `S ⊇ T_s`, then `K := I^z_S` disintegrates `C`. *Proof:* let `σ := ω_K ⊕ ω'_{I∖K}` for `ω,ω' ∈ C`. Prove by induction on a topological order of `G`, for every `v`:
```
(T_v ⊆ S      → X_v(σ) = X_v(ω))    ∧    (T_v ∩ S = ∅ → X_v(σ) = X_v(ω')).
```
For `v ∈ Z`, Z-closure gives one of the two antecedents, so `X_v(σ) = z_v`; the induction step goes through because `p ∈ pa(v) ∖ Z ⟹ T_p ⊆ T_v`, `p ∈ pa(v) ∩ Z ⟹ X_p(σ) = z_p` (already proved for `p`), and the consulted entry `(v, X_pa(v)(σ))` then lies in `I^z_{T_v}`, hence on the correct side of the splice. Together with (L3⇐) this gives **`H(X_s|z) ⊆ I^z_{S_Z(s)}`** — the whole "easy" upper bound.

**(L5) Propagation / all-or-nothing (the hard lemma).** If `J` disintegrates `C` and `w ∈ Z`, then `J ∩ I^z_{T_w} = ∅` or `I^z_{T_w} ⊆ J`.
*Reduction:* call `i ~ k` (for `i ≠ k ∈ I`) if there are `ω, ω' ∈ C` differing **exactly** at `{i,k}` such that one of the two mixed points is outside `C`. If `i ~ k` then no disintegrating `J` separates `i` from `k` (the mixed point *is* the splice, because `ω, ω'` agree everywhere else). So it suffices that `I^z_{T_w}` is **~-connected**.
*Connectivity:* (a) *Adjacent step:* for `u ∈ pa(v) ∖ Z`, `v ∈ T_w`, and any entries `(u,y) ∈ I^z_u`, `(v,y') ∈ I^z_v` realizable in one global assignment, `(u,y) ~ (v,y')`. Witness: constants everywhere except — `t_u[y] = a₀` vs `a₁ = y'_u` (`a₀ ≠ a₁`, needs `|Val_u| ≥ 2`), `t_v` sensitive only to its `u`-coordinate, and a copy chain `v ⇝ w` ending in `t_w = z_w` exactly on the live branch; all other `w' ∈ Z` get constant tables `z_{w'}`. The mixed point that takes `t_u[y] = a₁` with the old `t_v` breaks `X_w = z_w`.
(b) *Same node:* all entries of `I^z_u` are mutually ~-connected, by induction on the topological order — each connects to some entry of a parent (roots have a singleton `I^z_u`), and the parent's entries are mutually connected by IH.
(c) `T_w` is ancestrally closed and every `u ∈ T_w` has a directed `G`-path to `w` inside `T_w`, so (a)+(b) connect the whole block.
*Verified:* exhaustively, over all `Z`, `z` and all disintegrating `J` for the five DAGs — 102 k checks, 0 violations.

**(L6) Assembly.** `H(X_s|z) ⊇ I^z_{T_s}` by (L3⇒); apply (L5) repeatedly (`Relation.ReflTransGen` over the "add `A_Z(w)`" step) to get `H ⊇ I^z_{S_Z(s)}`; with (L4) this is equality. For sets, `H(X_A|z) = ⋃_{a∈A} H(X_a|z)` is the paper's Lemma 4.8 (already formalized as `history_pair`). Corollary 2 follows because the `I^z_u` are nonempty and pairwise disjoint.

## 2. From Theorem 1 to Prop 5.5: the graph-theoretic half

> **Theorem 3.** For `s,t ∈ V`: `S_Z(s) ∩ S_Z(t) ≠ ∅` **iff** there is an active trail between `s` and `t` given `Z`.

**Convention (important, and a paper gap).** The paper never defines d-separation (it cites Koller–Friedman) yet states Prop 5.5 for *arbitrary* `V₁,V₂,V₃ ⊆ V`. Its own remark in the proof of Prop 5.7 ("the path from `v₂` to itself has zero edges and can only be blocked by itself") fixes the reading that makes Prop 5.5 true: **endpoints count as non-collider nodes of the trail, so a trail is blocked if any of its nodes — endpoints included — is a non-collider lying in `Z`** (the zero-edge trail `v–v` is blocked iff `v ∈ Z`). Under this convention I checked `S_Z`-separation against trail enumeration on 400 random DAGs (3–5 nodes), all `V₁,V₂` of size ≤ 2 and all `V₃` of size ≤ 2: **649 584 triples, 0 mismatches.** Under the competing convention (endpoint with an incoming edge treated as a collider) the equivalence fails massively. This should be recorded as a formalization decision / erratum.

**Easy direction (active trail ⟹ intersection).** Decompose the active trail at its colliders `c₁,…,c_k`; between consecutive colliders it is a fork `c_{i} ← … ← f_i → … → c_{i+1}` whose interior avoids `Z`. Then `f_i ∈ A_Z(c_i) ∩ A_Z(c_{i+1})`, and each collider `c` has `A_Z(c) ⊆ A_Z(d)` for some `d ∈ Z` (take the first `Z`-node on a directed path from `c` into `Z`; `c ∈ An(Z) ∪ Z` is exactly the collider condition). Z-closure of `S_Z(s)` then swallows `A_Z(d_1), A_Z(d_2), …` in turn, and the last fork peak `f_k` lies in `S_Z(s) ∩ A_Z(t) ⊆ S_Z(s) ∩ S_Z(t)`. ~1 page of graph theory, no walk/path subtleties.

**Hard direction (intersection ⟹ active trail).** Induct on the closure: every witness added to `S_Z(s)` carries a certificate `s ⇄ u` that is active and leaves `u` forwards; the closure step `A_Z(w) ⊆ S` glues `s ⇄ m`, `m ⇝ w`, `w ⇜ u` with `m` a fork (`m ∉ Z`) and `w` a collider (`w ∈ Z`). The glue produces a **walk**; converting it to a trail is the one genuinely fiddly step. It is doable by the standard shortest-walk argument (if `x` repeats, shortcut; the only dangerous case — `x` non-collider at both occurrences but a collider in the shortcut — is safe because the returning sub-walk must itself contain a collider `c ∈ An(Z)∪Z` reached from `x` by a directed path, so `x ∈ An(Z)`). Zhang et al. avoid the generic lemma by a bespoke recursive construction (their §6, Lemma 6.1); either way, budget for it.

**Prop 5.5** = Corollary 2 + Theorem 3 + "d-separation of sets = pairwise d-separation of members" (trails have single-vertex endpoints).

## 3. Cost of the paper's literal route

The proof in Appendix B.3 chains: (i) K–F soundness **and completeness** of d-separation, (ii) Lemma 5.3 (`τ` bijection), (iii) Theorem 6.2.

* (ii) and (iii) are needed for §5–§6 anyway, so they are not marginal cost.
* **K–F soundness** (d-sep ⟹ CI in every `P` factorizing over `G`) needs the whole probabilistic Markov layer over DAGs: factorization, marginalization over topological orders, and either the moral-ancestral argument (i.e. *also* the graph theory of §2 above) or a semi-graphoid derivation. **≈800–1 500 lines.**
* **K–F completeness** (not d-separated ⟹ some factorizing `P` exhibits dependence) is the hard half and the reason to avoid this route. Textbook proofs are either sketchy (K–F Thm 3.4 leans on a "for almost all parameterizations" argument, essentially Meek 1995) or require constructing a distribution along an active path and computing a strict inequality of probabilities. Nothing in Mathlib helps. **≈1 500–4 000 lines, high variance, and the statement one is proving is the classical theorem, not the paper's.** I found no existing mechanization of d-separation soundness/completeness in Lean or Isabelle; the only mechanized d-separation result I could find is Zhang et al.'s Rocq development of a *noninterference* characterization (arXiv:2604.22041) — i.e. the direct route, not the probabilistic one.

Marginal cost, paper route: **≈2 300–5 500 lines**, worst-case unbounded, and it drags a large probabilistic detour through a proposition that is purely combinatorial.

**Hybrid worth knowing about (fallback).** The hard analytic lemma (L5) can be replaced by: "given an active trail, construct one explicit product distribution on `Ω` making `X_s, X_t` dependent given `z`", combined with Lemma 6.3 (soundness for events — the *easy* half of Thm 6.2, needed for §6 regardless). This trades splice combinatorics for finite-sum arithmetic over a hand-built distribution. It is *not* obviously cheaper (the same copy-chain construction reappears, plus probability), but it is a genuine escape hatch if (L5) turns out worse in Lean than expected.

Calibration: §4 + Appendix A + Lemma B.1 + Appendix C.1 of this same paper is already formalized in this repo in **608 lines** (`FactoredSpaces/{Basic,History,Independence}.lean`) — but that material is definitional and its proofs are two-rewrite affairs. Prop 5.5 is the first place in the paper where a real construction is required, so a 3–4× multiple over a §4-sized chunk is the right prior. All of `Disintegrates`, `Generates`, `history`, `Finset.piecewise` splicing and Lemmas 4.7–4.9 are already available, which is exactly the API items 4–7 consume.

**Confidence:** ~70 % that items 3–11 land under 3 000 lines; ~85 % under 4 000 including items 1–2. The mathematics is settled (verified exhaustively, and the analogous theorem has been mechanized in Rocq by an independent group), so the residual risk is Lean-mechanical, not mathematical.

**Top risks.**

1. **(L5) in Lean.** The index type is dependent (`(v, y : Val_pa(v))`), so "define a table by cases on the entry and evaluate `X_w` along a path" involves `DecidableEq` on a sigma type and transport of parent-assignment equalities. The math is one page; the Lean could be 900. *Mitigation:* factor out a single reusable `constantTable`/`copyChain` API with its evaluation lemmas (`X_v (copyChain p ω) = …`) and use it for items 6 and 7 both; if it still bites, fall back to the probabilistic hybrid of §3.
2. **Trails and walk→trail (item 10).** Mathlib has no directed-trail-with-orientation API and `SimpleGraph.Walk.bypass` does not preserve activity, so the shortcut case analysis must be done by hand. Underestimating this by 2× is easy. *Mitigation:* represent a trail as a `List (V × Bool)` with an invariant rather than an inductive family; or route through the moral-ancestral graph (Lauritzen et al. 1990), where the undirected side can borrow `SimpleGraph.Reachable`.
3. (Minor, but it costs review time rather than lines.) The endpoint convention of §2 must be decided and disclosed; the paper does not define d-separation at all, and Prop 5.5 is false for overlapping `V₁,V₂,V₃` under the other natural reading.

---

## 5. Verdict

**Direct route: bounded and tractable.** Take it. It needs no probability theory whatsoever, it produces a *stronger* and reusable artifact (a closed-form conditional history for `M_G`, which also gives Prop 5.6 and the Prop 5.7 perfect-map argument for free), and it decouples Prop 5.5 from Theorem 6.2 entirely — worth something for the dependency graph of the formalization. Do **not** formalize K–F completeness; that is the one item in this neighbourhood with genuinely unbounded risk.
