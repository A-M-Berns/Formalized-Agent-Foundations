# Spike: how hard is *Factored Space Models* (arXiv:2412.02579)?

**Paper.** Garrabrant, Mayer, Wache, Lang, Eisenstat, Dell, *Factored space models:
Towards causality between levels of abstraction*, arXiv:2412.02579v2 (20 Dec 2024),
cs.AI. Explicitly "a reframing of finite factored set models [Garrabrant 2109.11513]" —
the paper this repo already has complete in `FiniteFactoredSets/`.

**Verdict.** Tractable, and smaller than Finite Factored Sets: **50 numbered nodes**
versus FFS's 98. The mathematics is entirely finite and elementary — no measure theory,
one polynomial-vanishing argument, one topological continuity argument. The section that
carries the paper's headline result (§6 + Appendix C) is long but has no conceptual
obstruction. **There is exactly one real problem, and it is §5.2–5.3: d-separation.**
Mathlib has no d-separation, no DAG theory, and barely any digraph theory, and the paper
*cites* the soundness and completeness of d-separation rather than proving it. That
touches 3 of the 50 nodes and should be split off as a second phase.

Artifacts from this spike:

- `FactoredSpaces/Spike.lean` — 362 lines, **compiles, zero `sorry`**, axioms clean
  (`propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound` only). It settles the encoding question and
  carries working proofs of Definitions 4.1/4.5/4.6/4.10/4.11, Lemmas 4.7, 4.8, 4.12
  (one direction), A.1, A.2, C.3, C.4 (i⟺iii), and the set-theoretic core of Lemma 6.3.
- `FactoredSpaces/spike-build.sh` — compiles the spike against the parent checkout's built oleans
  (~30 s) so the worktree needs no Mathlib build of its own.

## 1. The headline finding: the encoding is not the hard part

Everything in this paper is stated over `Ω = ×_{i∈I} Ω_i` with projections `π_J`, a
merge `a · b` of families over disjoint index sets, and Cartesian products of
projections `C_J × C_{I∖J}`. The obvious Lean rendering — `(∀ i : ↥J, Ω i)` with
transport across `↥(J ∪ K) ≃ ↥J ⊕ ↥K` — would make the whole development a
dependent-subtype slog.

It is avoidable. The paper's disintegration condition

> `J` disintegrates `C` iff `C = C_J × C_{I∖J}`

is **equivalent to closure of `C` under splicing two of its own members at `J`**, i.e.
`∀ a b ∈ C, J.piecewise a b ∈ C`, where `Finset.piecewise` is Mathlib's dependent
`if i ∈ J then a i else b i`. No subtypes, no transport. This equivalence is *proved*
in the spike (`disintegrates_iff_prod`), against a faithful subtype-free rendering of
the paper's `C_J × C_{I∖J}` — it is the load-bearing claim of the whole design and I did
not want it on assertion.

Downstream payoff, measured rather than guessed:

| Paper result | Paper's proof | Spike |
|---|---|---|
| Lemma A.1 (disintegration closed under ∩, ∪) | ~1 page, auxiliary 4-fold product `C^×` | 2 lines each, after two `piecewise` rewrites |
| Lemma A.2 (generation closed under ∩) | ~½ page | ~8 lines |
| Lemma 4.7 (history is the minimum) | intersection-closure + finiteness | ~15 lines + one generic finite-lattice lemma |
| Lemma 4.8 (history of a joint variable) | ~½ page | ~15 lines |
| Lemma C.4 (i⟺iii) | ~½ page | ~25 lines |
| Lemma 6.3, set-theoretic half | 4-line display | ~10 lines |

The two `piecewise` rewrites that do all the work are worth recording:
`(J ∪ K).piecewise a b = J.piecewise a (K.piecewise a b)` and
`(J ∩ K).piecewise a b = J.piecewise (K.piecewise a b) b`.

**Where the subtypes come back:** sums and products over factors — marginals
`P_J = ⨂_{i∈J} P_i`, outer products (Def C.1/C.2), Lemma C.15. Splitting
`∑_{ω : Pt Ω}` into `∑_{α : Ω_J} ∑_{β : Ω_{I∖J}}` genuinely needs
`Equiv.piEquivPiSubtypeProd` (verified present in the pinned Mathlib). This is the top
open risk: the set algebra wants the splice encoding, the measure algebra wants the
product encoding, and the bridge lemma between them has to be built once and used
everywhere. I did **not** probe it. If something bites in this project, it bites there.

## 2. Node inventory

Fifty numbered nodes, on a **shared section-scoped counter**
(`\newtheorem{theorem}{Theorem}[section]`, everything else `[theorem]`, appendix sections
lettered) — the same scheme as `ModalAgents`, so `scripts/check-modal-agents-nodes.py` is
the template for the new checker, not the FFS or Cartesian Frames one.

```
Definitions 14 · Lemmas 28 · Propositions 6 · Theorem 1 · Corollary 1
§4 Factored space models        4.1–4.12   (12)
§5 Bayes nets and FSMs          5.1–5.8    ( 8)
§6 Soundness and completeness   6.1–6.6    ( 6)
App A  proofs for §4            A.1–A.2    ( 2)
App B  proofs for §5            B.1–B.2    ( 2)
App C  proofs for §6            C.1–C.20   (20)
```

45 of 50 carry a LaTeX `\label`; **5 do not** — Definition 4.2 (Factored Space, the
paper's central object), Definition 5.1, Proposition 5.6, Definition 5.7, Definition C.6.
So, as in Cartesian Frames and FFS, the **printed number is the provenance key**, uniformly.
The source is committed at `FactoredSpaces/notes/` (to be added) from arXiv e-print;
`main.tex` + `meta/environment.tex` + `tables/` + `images/`.

## 3. Difficulty by tranche

**T1 — §4 + Appendix A + Lemmas C.3/C.4 (14 nodes). Easy; effectively done.**
The spike covers all of it except Lemma 4.9 (history of a variable = union of histories
of its values; a `Finset` induction using 4.8, routine) and Definitions 4.2/4.3/4.4,
which need the distribution layer. Estimate ~700 lines. Low risk.

**T2 — the finite-probability substrate (Def 4.3, 4.4, 6.1, C.1, C.2, C.5, C.11, C.15,
C.16). Medium; the real risk.** Distributions on `Pt Ω` as `∏_i P_i`, marginals, outer
products, supports, delta distributions, conditional probability with the paper's
`P(C) = 0 ⇒ independence holds` convention. `FiniteFactoredSets/Probability.lean`'s
`ProbDist` is a usable *pattern* but not reusable code (FFS factors are `Setoid S`
partitions; FSM factors are literal product coordinates). Estimate ~900–1200 lines.

**T3 — §6 soundness + local-to-global (6.3, 6.5, 6.6, C.5). Low–medium.**
Lemma 6.3's set half is done; the measure half is short once T2 exists. Lemma 6.5 is
"a polynomial with infinitely many roots is zero" — `Polynomial.eq_zero_of_infinite_isRoot`
is present, and this repo has *already solved the analogous step* in
`FiniteFactoredSets/Probability.lean` (`eq_zero_of_eval_pos_eq_zero`, via
`MvPolynomial.funext_set`). Estimate ~400 lines.

**T4 — Appendix C.3, completeness (6.4, C.6–C.20; 15 nodes). The bulk. Medium–high, by
volume not by depth.** The paper's own "most technical contribution": cohistory,
mutual exclusion (C.9), an interpolation-plus-polynomial argument (C.7), progressive
factor replacement with support side-conditions (C.12), delta-distribution arguments
(C.18, C.8). No deep mathematics; a great deal of `P(C) > 0` bookkeeping, which is
exactly the kind of thing that inflates a Lean development. Estimate ~1500–2500 lines.
This is where the schedule risk lives.

**T5 — §5 and Appendix B. Split it in two.**
- *Without d-separation* (Def 5.1, Prop 5.2, Lemma 5.3, Prop 5.4, Prop 5.6, B.1, B.2 —
  7 nodes): the DAG-to-FSM construction, `τ` bijective, the factorization property, and
  the ancestor relation. Needs a DAG with parents/ancestors (`Relation.TransGen` suffices)
  and the well-founded recursion defining `X_v` (Eq. 4) over an acyclic graph — moderate
  Lean friction, no missing theory. Prop 5.2's axioms 1–4 are proved in the paper by
  citing Pearl ("independence is a semigraphoid"), but the paper also says they follow
  directly; **prove them directly from the history definition and the citation
  disappears.** Estimate ~800 lines.
- *Needing d-separation* (Prop 5.5, Def 5.7, Prop 5.8 — 3 nodes): **defer.** See below.

**T6 — witnesses / non-vacuity.** Prop 5.8(2)'s counterexample (`Ω = Ω₁ = {0,1,2}`,
`X₂ = [U₁ > 0]`, histories `{1}, {1}, {1}, ∅`) is a concrete, cheap non-vacuity witness in
the splice encoding. §3's "no perfect map exists" argument is unnumbered prose and needs
d-separation, so it rides with T5b.

## 4. The one real problem: d-separation

Confirmed by grep against the pinned Mathlib: `Mathlib/Combinatorics/Digraph/` contains
exactly `Basic.lean` and `Orientation.lean`. There are no directed walks, no
reachability, no DAG predicate, no Bayesian networks, and no d-separation anywhere in
Mathlib. Everything §5.2–5.3 needs about graphs would be built from zero — trails in the
underlying undirected graph, colliders, blocking, d-separation itself: call it 800–1500
lines with a usable API, a small library of its own.

That is merely work. The actual problem is one step up. **Proposition 5.5's proof cites
the soundness and completeness of d-separation (Koller–Friedman [15]) as an external
theorem**; the paper does not prove it, and it is a serious result in its own right —
formalizing it is comparable in size to formalizing the rest of this paper. Three ways out,
and this is a ruling for Anson, not a decision I should make:

1. **Disclose it.** State d-sep soundness/completeness as a named hypothesis or a
   documented type-`(c)` boundary, exactly as the paper depends on it. Cheap, honest,
   and structurally identical to what `ModalAgents/` does with its `PA+1`/`PA+2` gap.
2. **Route around it.** The paper remarks that Prop 5.5 "is also possible to prove
   directly without Theorem 6.2". A direct combinatorial proof of "`V₁`, `V₂` d-separated
   by `V₃` ⟺ `X_{V₁} ⊥_Ω X_{V₂} | X_{V₃}`" would make §5 self-contained — replacing an
   unbounded citation with bounded work. **Worth an hour of investigation before
   committing to option 1**; I have not checked whether the direct argument really avoids
   the completeness half.
3. **Prove d-sep soundness/completeness.** A separate project. Not this one.

## 5. Reuse from `FiniteFactoredSets/`

Less than the "reframing" framing suggests. The two developments share vocabulary
(history, orthogonality/structural independence, time) but not objects: FFS factors are
partitions `Setoid S`, FSM factors are literal coordinates of a Pi type. **No code
transfers.** What does transfer:

- the elementary-probability *pattern* (`ProbDist` as a structure on `Set S → ℝ`);
- the polynomial-vanishing analytic step, already solved;
- the whole provenance apparatus — a new `scripts/check-factored-spaces-nodes.py` cloned
  from the ModalAgents checker (same counter scheme), registration in `scripts/papers.py`,
  `AxiomAudit` inventory, trust-surface section;
- the standing house rules on disclosure, which this paper will need for the d-separation
  boundary.

## 6. Faithfulness notes found while reading

- **Lemma C.3 is false as printed in one corner.** `X ▷_C Y ⟺ (∀ ω,ω' ∈ C, X ω = X ω' →
  Y ω = Y ω')` needs `Val(Y)` inhabited — the paper gets it silently from "let `f(x)` be
  arbitrary". If `C = ∅`, `Val(X)` nonempty and `Val(Y)` empty, (ii) is vacuous but no
  `f : Val(X) → Val(Y)` exists. `[Nonempty β]` fixes it and is harmless; it must be
  disclosed, not slipped in. (Recorded at the declaration in `Spike.lean`.)
- Definition 4.2 does not require the factors `Ω_i` to be nonempty, so `Ω` may be empty;
  several later arguments implicitly assume a point exists. Worth a systematic pass.
- Definition 6.1's `P(C) = 0 ⇒ independent` convention is deliberate and load-bearing
  (it is what makes the intersection axiom fail); encode it exactly as the product
  identity `P(A∩C)P(B∩C) = P(A∩B∩C)P(C)`, never as the conditional form.

## 7. Recommended scope, and an estimate I'll stand behind

**Phase 1 (recommended): 47 of 50 nodes** — all of §4, §6, Appendices A, B, C, and the
d-separation-free part of §5. No external-citation boundary anywhere in it, and it
contains Theorem 6.2, the paper's headline result. Roughly **4300–5800 lines**, i.e.
**60–75% of the FiniteFactoredSets effort**, with the risk concentrated in T4's volume
and in the T2 splice/product bridge.

**Phase 2: the remaining 3 nodes** (Prop 5.5, Def 5.7, Prop 5.8) behind a purpose-built
d-separation library, with the Koller–Friedman citation either discharged by a direct
proof or disclosed as a boundary. Size this only after investigating option 2 above.

**Before writing anything else, do this:** build the T2 measure/marginal layer against
the splice encoding and prove Lemma C.15 (`P(A_J × A_{I∖J}) = P_J(A_J)·P_{I∖J}(A_{I∖J})`).
It is a day of work and it is the only remaining unknown in Phase 1. Everything else in
this report is either measured or routine.
