## Finding 1 — the definition of factorization carries a nontriviality condition

**This is the one that would have bitten.** The obvious rendering of `F = (S,B)` —
finite `S`, `B` a finite set of partitions whose joint-coordinate map is a bijection —
is *weaker than the paper's*. Definition 10 *defines* a factorization as "a set `B ⊆ parts(S)` of **nontrivial**
partitions of `S`" such that `π` is bijective, Theorem 1 repeats the condition, and
Corollary 1 (`basisdisjoint`) is proved from it.

Dropping it is not harmless. For a one-element `S` the indiscrete partition is a legal
factor under the weak definition, so both `{}` and `{Ind}` factor `S` — which
contradicts Proposition 5's claim that the trivial factorization is *unique*. The Lean
structure therefore needs the third field:

```lean
structure FFS (S : Type*) [Fintype S] where
  B : Finset (Setoid S)
  bij : Function.Bijective (fun (s : S) (b : B) => Quotient.mk (b : Setoid S) s)
  nontrivial : ∀ b ∈ B, ∃ s t : S, ¬ b s t
```

This is a definition that looks right, elaborates, and quietly admits objects the paper
excludes — exactly the failure mode the repo standard exists to catch, found only by
reading Theorem 2's statement rather than the definition environment.

## Finding 2 — the modeling decisions are cheap and settle cleanly

* `B : Finset (Setoid S)` rather than an indexed family. Load-bearing: §5.2's `Irr^F(E)`
  is a partition **of `B` itself**, which needs `B` to be a set.
* Polynomial variables `𝒫(S)` render as `Finset S`. Equivalent to `Set S` for
  `Fintype S`, and hands you `DecidableEq` on variables for free. Benign; disclose it.
* The chimera function is `coord.symm` of a pointwise `if`, where `coord` is
  `Equiv.ofBijective` of the factorization map. Proposition 4's clauses 1–2 fall out in
  three lines each.
* Non-vacuity is discharged by construction (`boolFFS`, the discrete factorization of
  `Bool`) and cost about fifteen lines with no dependent-rewrite fight. This should
  scale to the §6 worked examples better than CF's `Frame` witnesses did.

## Finding 3 — `factor2`'s hard step is ~30 lines, and it is generic

The paper's "there can be no combining like terms, then, in the product `pq`" is the
statement that when `p` and `q` have disjoint variable sets, the addition map on
supports is injective and coefficient-multiplicative. Proved as
`coeff_add_mul_of_split`: split the antidiagonal, recover `a` from `a + b` by
`Finsupp.filter`, done. Nothing FFS-specific — it belongs in a support module and is
plausibly upstreamable.

`factor2` step (i) — factors of `Q^F_E` share no variable — is proved in full
(`vars_disjoint_of_mul_eq_Q`), routed through:

* `eq_of_part_eq` = Corollary 1, proved via the chimera as the paper does;
* `degreeOf_mono_le` — `Q^F_E` is multilinear, which is *precisely* where Corollary 1
  does its work, and hence where Finding 1's `nontrivial` field is consumed;
* `Q_ne_zero` — cheapest via `eval (fun _ => 1) = E.card`, not coefficient bookkeeping.

Mathlib supplies more than expected: `degreeOf_mul_eq` and `degreeOf_prod_eq`
(`Algebra/MvPolynomial/NoZeroDivisors.lean`), `degreeOf_X`, `mem_vars_iff_degreeOf_ne_zero`.

**Revised risk assessment: `factor2` is no longer the research-grade risk it looked
like.** The remaining work (all coefficients equal `r` and `1/r`; every term of `p` lies
in `monos^F_C(E)`) is bookkeeping on top of `coeff_add_mul_of_split`, not a new idea.

## Finding 4 — the Fundamental Theorem's analytic step is a one-liner

Theorem 3's "`q` is zero on an open subset of inputs, so `q` is the zero polynomial" is
`MvPolynomial.funext_set` at `Set.Ioi 0` (`eq_zero_of_eval_pos_eq_zero`, 4 lines).

Also confirmed by reading §5.4–5.5: the paper defines a probability distribution
elementarily as a finitely-additive `P : 𝒫(S) → ℝ` and states the theorem division-free
as `P(x∩z)·P(y∩z) = P(x∩y∩z)·P(z)`. So the whole probabilistic half can be formalized
**verbatim, with no measure theory and no type-`(c)` substitution**. A bridge to
Mathlib's `condIndepFun` would be extra credit and must stay a separate labeled lemma,
never a stand-in.

## Gotcha for the log

`exact absurd (Finset.mem_antidiagonal.2 rfl) h` loops in `whnf` (200k heartbeats) on
the `Finsupp` antidiagonal instance. `simp only [Finset.mem_antidiagonal] at h` first is
instant. Recorded at the site in `Spike.lean`.

Also: `Setoid.trans`/`symm`/`refl` take the setoid as an *instance* argument, so any
proof with two setoids in scope resolves to the wrong one. Use the primed
`b.trans'`/`b.symm'`/`b.refl'` throughout — this will be pervasive in FFS, which is
about families of setoids by construction.
