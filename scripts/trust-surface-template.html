Formalized Agent Foundations — the trust-surface read-through

Every paper node beside the Lean statement that carries it, for every paper this repository formalizes. The kernel has already checked every proof body; what it cannot check is whether each statement says what the paper says. That judgment requires a human reader — this page is the map for making it.

What a read-through certifies

A kernel-clean proof certifies that the body matches the statement, and nothing more. The statement — its definitions, hypotheses, and conclusion — is the trust surface. Reading a node means holding the two panes side by side and asking four questions:

1 · Fidelity. Does the Lean statement say what the paper node says — same quantifiers, same conclusion shape, no strengthened premise doing hidden work?
2 · Satisfiability. Is every hypothesis inhabitable? Constructed witnesses are named in the audit notes; a premise with no possible inhabitant makes the theorem vacuously true, and this failure mode has occurred (and been repaired) before.
3 · No smuggling. Is the conclusion (or the forcing step) taken as a hypothesis anywhere? Structures' fields are hypotheses too — see the field-freeze appendices.
4 · Metering. Where a paper's own statement quantifies over an efficiency class, check which class the Lean statement actually uses; the classes are not interchangeable, and the narrower ones appear only where a node is marked qualified.

What is on each card, and what is not

Every card is the same mechanical pair: the paper's printed statement — machine-converted from the committed TeX, or, where no TeX source for the paper exists, shown verbatim as extracted from the committed PDF — beside the exact source text of the Lean statement (or statements) annotated with that node. Beyond that pair, the papers carry different editorial material, because different material exists — and the difference is real, not a presentational choice:

Only Logical Induction has a strength classification and per-node reading notes. They are the product of a dedicated re-derivation pass and a machine-checked table; the other libraries have had no such pass. No tier, audit note or reading note is shown for a paper that does not have one — the correspondence is what those sections claim, and it is all they claim.

How to use this page

Within each paper, nodes appear in that paper's own order, grouped by its own sections; definitions come first and are load-bearing for everything after. Where a node has several inventory endpoints, the ‹ › arrows page through all of them. Check-boxes track progress across every formalized paper and persist locally in the browser. Signatures are statements only, verbatim from source; file paths locate the full declaration. Paper statements are machine-converted from the arXiv TeX (custom macros expanded, rendered as MathML) — for any formula that looks off, the arXiv PDF is the authority; the Lean pane is exact source text.

Generated from the repository by scripts/gen-trust-surface.py, driven by the paper registry scripts/papers.py; node locations come from scripts/paper_nodes.py, the same module the per-paper provenance checkers use. The page carries a hash over every input — each paper's source, every library's Lean sources, the registry and the prose files quoted here — and scripts/check_trust_surface.py fails CI if it is stale.

Logical Induction

Garrabrant, Benson-Tilsen, Critch, Soares, Taylor (2016) · arXiv:1609.03543v5 · LogicalInduction/ · %%NLI%% nodes

The standing disclosures (apply to every node)

Five things get called "a boundary" and they are not the same: a modeling substitution (a weaker or different object standing in for the paper's — the only one that is a debt against faithfulness), a representation interface (a choice of how to present a faithful object, restricting who may supply the input without changing what is proved), a paper erratum, a strengthening, and certification technology. Sorted that way: sentences are propositional (Formula ℕ), which is the paper's own outer language by its Notation section, with the first-order Θ entering through explicit interfaces; the trader class is ordinary machine polynomial time (MachineEfficientTrader, through Complexity.FP) and the fuel-clocked dd:fuel calculus is certification technology proved to land inside it; dd:fuel on a statement's own data sequences is a representation interface, charged once at def:ec and not again downstream. Details: README.md and LogicalInduction/README.md.

Status — is the paper's printed statement right, and do we prove it?

exact%%NEXACT%% — the printed statement, proved, on the paper's own hypotheses
strengthened%%NSTR%% — the printed statement and more: a weaker hypothesis, a stronger conclusion, or a datum the paper assumes and we construct
corrected%%NCORR%% — the printed statement is defective; the repaired statement is proved, and the note names the erratum
refuted%%NREF%% — the printed statement is false, and its negation is proved here
qualified%%NQUAL%% — falls short: a restricted class, a retained interface, or the intended subject matter abstracted to a placeholder; the note says which

The secondary universal / instantiated badge says which inductors — every one, or the constructed LIA with its obligations discharged. Neither is stronger than the other, and neither overrides the status.

Status, axis, note and the endpoints shown below them are all read from one file, scripts/coverage-classification.md, and scripts/check_endpoint_coverage.py fails the build unless every curated endpoint resolves, carries the node's label, and is inside AxiomAudit.lean's LI-CANONICAL block. They used to be three independently maintained artifacts; every disagreement they developed was invisible. The refuted, corrected and qualified rows deserve the slow pass.

What the Lean pane shows

Only the canonical endpoints — the small curated set a skeptical reader is asked to read, the paper's own printed form first. It is not every declaration carrying the label: thm:scon has 54 axiom-checked carriers and 4 canonical endpoints. The rest are listed by name under the slides, and remain under the build's axiom gate as internal regression assertions; they are construction machinery and compatibility carriers, not the read-through set.

Metering vocabulary

RpnSentenceCodes / token-metered classes are the faithful rendering of the paper's def:ec; whole-value classes (PolyNatCodes &c.) are provably narrower and appear only where a row is marked qualified.

Cartesian Frames

Garrabrant, Herrmann, Lopez-Wild (2021) · arXiv:2109.10996v1 · CartesianFrames/ · %%NCF%% nodes

No strength classification exists for this paper. There is no per-node tier and no per-node audit note below, because neither has been produced: the dedicated re-derivation pass and the machine-checked strength table that Logical Induction has were never run here. What the cards below assert is the correspondence — this printed node, these Lean statements — and nothing about how strong each rendering is. Read the statements.

Scope

All 60 numbered nodes of the paper carry Lean statements, across the main text and both appendices, with no modeling substitutions. Numbered nodes with no Lean statement: %%CF_MISSING%%. One node is formalized only in part, by user ruling — its card says so, in the ruling's own words.

How nodes are cited

Most nodes of this paper carry no LaTeX \label, so the printed Definition n / Claim n / Theorem n is the provenance key. scripts/check-cartesian-frames-nodes.py validates every annotation against the committed TeX fail-closed, and requires each annotated declaration to be listed in AxiomAudit.lean's CF-INVENTORY block. The cards below are built from those same annotations, so a node's Lean pane shows exactly the declarations that claim it.

Where the printed proof is the thing that is wrong

Defects found in this paper during formalization are recorded in CartesianFrames/notes/paper-errata.md; a node with an erratum carries it on its card. Consult it before concluding that a Lean proof diverges from the printed one — in several places the statement is sound and the printed proof is not.

Robust Cooperation in the Prisoner's Dilemma

Barász, Christiano, Fallenstein, Herreshoff, LaVictoire, Yudkowsky (2014) · arXiv:1401.5577 · ModalAgents/ · %%NMA%% nodes

No strength classification exists for this paper. As with Cartesian Frames, the cards below carry no tier and no per-node audit note, because none has been produced. They assert the correspondence only.

Scope — and what is deliberately absent

The cooperation results are carried at the Gödel–Löb level; the §1 and §4 nodes that quantify over arbitrary agents are carried at the arithmetic level they are printed at, so CliqueBot and its separation from the modal agents are in scope. Node-completeness is still not claimed. The nodes with no Lean statement annotated to them, computed from the source and the annotations: %%MA_MISSING%%. That is Theorem 4.6, on modal agents that may refer to their own action — a genuine gap, and the only numbered node of the paper with no Lean statement (see ModalAgents/README.md). The provenance checker deliberately does not check the converse direction, so this page asserts nothing further about it.

How nodes are cited

The paper labels only 22 of its nodes and leaves several formalized ones unlabeled, so the provenance key is the printed number, recomputed from the committed TeX by emulating the paper's shared section-scoped theorem counter (scripts/check-modal-agents-nodes.py). Note that the paper's definition environment is an uncounted trivlist: this paper numbers no definitions at all, so no definition can be cited and none appears below. The definitional content — modal agents, the four concrete agents — lives in Lean declarations attached to the theorems that use them.

Inventoried endpoints that deliberately carry no annotation

These endpoints are axiom-checked in AxiomAudit.lean's MA-INVENTORY block but claim no numbered node, because the fact each proves is unnumbered paper prose or a step inside a proof. The list is computed from the inventory; each reason is quoted from ModalAgents/README.md.

Temporal Inference with Finite Factored Sets

Garrabrant (2021) · arXiv:2109.11513 · FiniteFactoredSets/ · %%NFFS%% nodes

This formalization is complete for its ruled scope, and no strength classification exists for this paper. The cards below cover §2 through §7 — partitions and factorizations, the chimera function, generation and history, orthogonality and time, subpartitions and conditional orthogonality, characteristic polynomials and their factorization, the fundamental theorem, temporal inference from orthogonality databases including both worked examples, and §7's embedded observations, counterfactability and conditional time. All 96 in-scope nodes are accounted for: 87 carry a Lean declaration of this project's and the remaining nine are rendered outright by Mathlib vocabulary (listed below), so 87 + 9 = 96. Two things are deliberately not claimed, and both are rulings rather than gaps: Conjecture 1 (§7.2) is stated as a Prop and deliberately not proved — no declaration anywhere in the library has that type — and Examples 3 and 4 are out of scope, both concerning infinite factored sets, which is the case the paper itself expects the fundamental theorem to fail in. The cards carry no tier and no per-node audit note, because none has been produced; they assert the correspondence only. Nodes with no Lean statement annotated to them: %%FFS_MISSING%%.

How nodes are cited

The paper carries only 32 \label commands, two of which are not on numbered nodes at all (one labels a table, one labels a section), so exactly 30 of its 98 numbered nodes are labelled — and the labels it does carry are working names (templabel1, templabel2), so the provenance key is the printed number, recomputed from the committed TeX (scripts/check-finite-factored-sets-nodes.py). Unlike the other counter-derived paper here, this one declares each environment without a [section] argument and without sharing counters, so every environment numbers independently and never resets: Definition 1…Definition 50 run the length of the paper alongside Proposition 1…Proposition 36. Note also that the source declares a lemma environment it never uses and a second lemma2 that also prints "Lemma" and carries all three printed Lemmas; the checker fails closed on that ambiguity rather than guessing.

Nodes rendered by Mathlib vocabulary rather than by a declaration

Nine nodes have no Lean carrier of this project's, because the modeling decisions in FiniteFactoredSets.lean render them with vocabulary Mathlib already has. Six are in §2.1: Definition 1 (the disjoint union ⊔S) is absorbed into Setoid and the dependent product, Definition 2 (partition) is Setoid, Definition 5 (∼_X) is the setoid relation, Definition 6 (finer) is ≤, Definition 7 (Dis_S/Ind_S) is ⊥/⊤, and Definition 9 (∏(B)) is the dependent product. The other three are outside §2.1: §5.1's Definition 29 (evaluation p(f)) is MvPolynomial.eval and Definition 30 (support supp(p)) is MvPolynomial.vars; §6.1's Definition 39 (preimages) is Set.preimage, with Setoid.comap for the partition clause. They are recorded in FiniteFactoredSets/README.md rather than inventoried, because there is no declaration of this project's to axiom-check.

Four nodes carried only in part

Four cards pair a whole printed node with a Lean statement covering part of it, on purpose, because the remaining clause is Mathlib's vocabulary rather than a declaration of this project's. Each is recorded in FiniteFactoredSets/README.md's table of Mathlib-rendered nodes.

  • Proposition 2, first sentence. bot_le_and_le_top carries the Proposition 2 annotation but states only that proposition's second sentence — the Dis_S / Ind_S bounds. Its first sentence, that the order is a partial order on Part(S), is Mathlib's PartialOrder (Setoid S) instance.
  • Definition 8, second sentence. The set form ⋁_S(C) is commonRefinement; the binary X ∨_S Y is Mathlib's X ⊓ Y, which is how §3 onward states it. The identification of the two is commonRefinement_pair, a bridge lemma rather than a node carrier.
  • Definition 15, third sentence. "Finite" and "finite-dimensional" factored sets are not a definition this library makes: under dd:finiteness-minimal they are the typeclass hypotheses [Finite S] and Finite F.B, carried on the individual statements that consume them.
  • Proposition 31's word "irreducible." The proposition itself is carried (irreducible_poly_of_mem_irr); it is the meaning of the word that is Mathlib's Irreducible, which over ℝ is strictly stronger than the paper's phrasing and agrees with it on every case the proposition asserts.

One inverted convention, stated once

The paper's order glyphs run opposite to Mathlib's: it writes X ≥_S Y for "X is finer than Y", so the paper's ≥_S is Mathlib's ≤ on Setoid, and Definition 8's common refinement ⋁_S(C) — a join in the paper's notation — is Mathlib's sInf. The Lean statements use Mathlib's convention throughout and never introduce the paper's glyphs, so no statement has to be read under two conventions at once. When comparing a card's paper statement against its Lean statement, this is the one place the two will look like they disagree when they do not.

Factored space models: Towards causality between levels of abstraction

Garrabrant, Mayer, Wache, Lang, Eisenstat, Dell (2024) · arXiv:2412.02579 · FactoredSpaces/ · %%NFSM%% nodes

This formalization is in progress, and no strength classification exists for this paper. The cards below are the correspondence view of what has landed so far — every paper node beside the Lean statement that carries it — and assert the correspondence only. Nodes with no Lean statement annotated to them yet: %%FSM_MISSING%%.

How nodes are cited

The paper labels 45 of its 50 numbered nodes and leaves five unlabeled — among them Definition 4.2, the factored space itself — so the provenance key is the printed number, recomputed from the committed TeX (scripts/check-factored-spaces-nodes.py). The source declares one section-scoped counter shared by theorem, definition, lemma, proposition and corollary (so numbers read Lemma 4.7, Definition 6.1), wraps several results in thmtools' restatable — counted where first stated, not where restated — and letters its appendix sections, so appendix nodes read Lemma A.1, Definition C.6.

Reading the statements

The factored space Ω = ×i∈I Ωi is the dependent function type Pt Ω; the paper's product CJ × CI∖J is stated literally (prodSplit) and worked with through its proved equivalent, closure under splicing two points at J (Finset.piecewise). The design decisions are tagged dd: in FactoredSpaces.lean.

Condensation: A Theory of Concepts

Eisenstat (2025) · OpenReview:HwKFJ3odui · Condensation/ · %%NCD%% nodes

This formalization is in progress, and no strength classification exists for this paper. It is at milestone M2 — a final statement and a proof for every in-scope node, every declaration axiom-clean — so a card appearing here asserts that the Lean statement shown carries that paper node and is proved; the badge on each declaration (axiom-clean) is the machine-checked confirmation. It is not yet registered as completed: the human statement read-through is outstanding. The legend below the fold says what each badge means and counts both. The paper's scope is settled at 42 numbered nodes; Examples 5.1–5.3 are proposed out of scope pending a ruling. There is no modelling substitution: Condensation.RVModel is Definition 3.1 verbatim, including its "with finite entropy" clause on the sample space. The narrowing dd:finite-range — every random variable of a model has finite range, rather than merely countable discrete range with finite entropy — was disclosed here until 2026-08-17 and was retired that day, when the substrate ShannonInformation/FiniteEntropy/ reached the corpus this development needs; Condensation/KNOWLEDGE.md's disclosures list is empty, and Condensation.Example.geomModel (a geometric variable on ℕ, provably not finite-range) is the witness that the model class genuinely grew. Numbered nodes with no Lean statement annotated to them: %%CD_MISSING%%.

How nodes are cited

This paper numbers its results through a single section-scoped counter shared by every environment — definitions, propositions, lemmas, theorems, corollaries and examples all draw from it — so node ids read Definition 2.1 through Corollary 5.10, and the printed number is the provenance key. What is different here is the source. This paper has no arXiv record and its LaTeX is not public, so unlike every other paper on this page there is no TeX in the repository to recompute numbers from. The committed source is Condensation/notes/condensation-25-07.txt, a pdftotext -layout extraction of the committed PDF, and scripts/check-condensation-nodes.py reads the printed numbers off its header lines. Because the extraction is therefore itself provenance-bearing, that checker additionally asserts that exactly the paper's 42 nodes are derived, so a re-extraction that mangles a header fails the build instead of quietly shrinking the set of nodes an annotation is allowed to name.

For the same reason the paper statements below are shown verbatim as extracted, in a preformatted block, rather than converted to typeset mathematics as the other four sections are: there is no markup to convert, and reflowing the text would mean rewriting the paper's statement. The extraction keeps the printed line breaks and the alignment of displayed equations, and the extractor's f-ligature slots are resolved back to letters; nothing else is altered. Where a statement is hard to read here, read the committed PDF.
