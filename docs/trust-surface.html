Logical Induction, formalized — the trust-surface read-through

Every paper node beside the Lean statement that carries it. The kernel has already checked every proof body; what it cannot check is whether each statement says what the paper says. That judgment requires a human reader — this page is the map for making it.

What a read-through certifies

A kernel-clean proof certifies that the body matches the statement, and nothing more. The statement — its definitions, hypotheses, and conclusion — is the trust surface. Reading a node means holding the two panes side by side and asking four questions:

1 · Fidelity. Does the Lean statement say what the paper node says — same quantifiers, same conclusion shape, no strengthened premise doing hidden work?
2 · Satisfiability. Is every hypothesis inhabitable? Constructed witnesses are named in the audit notes; a premise with no possible inhabitant makes the theorem vacuously true, and this failure mode has occurred (and been repaired) before.
3 · No smuggling. Is the conclusion (or the forcing step) taken as a hypothesis anywhere? Structures' fields are hypotheses too — see the field-freeze appendix.
4 · Metering. RpnSentenceCodes / symbol-metered classes are the faithful rendering of the paper's def:ec; whole-value classes (PolyNatCodes &c.) are provably narrower and appear only where a row is marked qualified.

The two standing disclosures (apply to every node)

Sentences are propositional (Formula ℕ); the paper's first-order Θ enters through explicit interfaces. Efficient computability is the fuel-clocked interpreter model dd:fuel, not a machine complexity class. "Paper strength" always means within this disclosed model. Details: LogicalInduction/README.md, "The two modeling boundaries".

def:tradestrat Trading Strategy paper strength · instantiated
Paper · arXiv:1609.03543v5

A trading strategy for day n, also called an n-strategy, is an affine combination of the form

T=c+ξ1ϕ1+⋯+ξkϕk,
where ϕ1,…,ϕk are sentences, ξ1,…,ξk are expressible features of rank ≤n, and
c=−∑iξi*nϕi
is a “cash term” recording the net cash flow when executing a transaction that buys ξi shares of ϕi for each i at the prevailing market price. (Buying negative shares is called “selling”.) We define T[1] to be c, and T[ϕ] to be the coefficient of ϕ in T, which is 0 if ϕ∉(ϕ1,…,ϕk). An n-strategy T can be encoded by the tuples (ϕ1,…ϕk) and (ξ1,…ξk) because the c term is determined by them. Explicitly, by linearity we have
T=ξ1·(ϕ1−*nϕ1)+⋯+ξk·(ϕk−*nϕk),
which means any n-strategy can be written as a linear combination of (ϕi−*nϕi) terms, each of which means “buy one share of ϕi at the prevailing price”.

def:luv Logically Uncertain Variable qualified
Paper · arXiv:1609.03543v5

A logically uncertain variable, abbreviated LUV, is any formula X free in one variable that defines a unique value via Γ, in the sense that

Γ⊢∃x:(X(x)∧∀x′:X(x′)→x′=x).
We refer to that value as the value of X. If Γ proves that the value of X is in [0,1], we call X a [0,1]-LUV.

𝕎(X):=sup{x∈[0,1]∣𝕎(“X≥x―”)=1}.
In other words, 𝕎(X) is the supremum of values that do not exceed X according to 𝕎. (This rather roundabout definition is necessary in cases where 𝕎 assigns X a non-standard value.)

We write 𝒰 for the set of all [0,1]-LUVs. When manipulating logically uncertain variables, we use shorthand like “X<0.5” for “∀x:X(x)→x<0.5”. See Secnotation for details.
